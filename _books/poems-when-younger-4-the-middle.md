<p class="verse">Remember me Pankaj, or is it too late,
Ten years is long indeed to forget an old mate.
You have left Benares behind, maybe the past is no more,
Yet one sudden morning, this shows up at your door!
But worry not my friend; I don't seek wealth, or a home safe to go,
But, this story I have to tell; and I just want you to know,
About how the world hurt me, and how I hurt it back,
About how I yearned to love, when love was all I lacked,
Because, at the end of this battle,
After all the blood you shed, and every tear you wipe,
You do long for a way to make sense of this life.

So it had begun with my birth, a &ldquo;middle class&rdquo; I was born,
You know the problem about being one is as if to remain one you have sworn!
You crave to reach the door out, in it an entire life you spend,
But it's the void in between where you keep dwelling till the end!
But birth was just the beginning; soon my father would be dead,
Dear uncles would grab his money, me and my mother betrayed,
With not a bread to eat, not a shed to live,
The world would get harsher, but we would still try to cope,
Because the middle class we are, we are not allowed to lose hope!

And so this kid would wake at dawn, for his age a bit too grown,
And sweat his life out at the factory, to make money of his own.
Carrying carpets heavier than his weight, and after spending hours in the dark,
At last he would stop to watch his friends, while coming back from his work,
Some playing marbles across the road, and some fishing along the pool,
He can't wait to watch these kids; he can't be late for his school.
He would write with the same fingers which had bled during the day.
Earning would become his entertainment, and getting to eat, his play.

You know, this kid never complained, nor wondered if life was fair,
Pankaj this kid, this was me, my childhood was never there.
But Pankaj, I made it in the end; the struggle didn't go in vain,
As I stood before the college entrance, I knew I would never look back again.
I thought I would change the world someday; my eyes had this spark,
And in this world full of strangers, I was sure I'll leave my mark.
But the world it's a large place, how little did I know,
For a layman stuck in Benares how good could it go!

We aspired of education and values &ndash; all obsolete at this hour;
Because the only path to thrive went through the people with power.
Soon a shady bungalow found me shaking hands with one of them
He thought his fake smile wouldn't tell me that I was just a pawn in their game
&ldquo;The deal is pretty simple&rdquo;, then his chair he would pull,
He said, &ldquo;You do me a favour, and I make your pockets full&rdquo;.
And I kept doing these favours; I was soon blinded by my greed,
I'd loot the debtors, knock the rebels, I'd make innocents bleed.
Soon threats changed to theft, the kidnap changed to kill,
If my morals tried to take me out, I was reminded of the deal.
Later, I would walk with my head sunk; meeting people had become tough,
Because I couldn't tell if I have killed someone they used to love.

But a part of me had known, that no matter where this path leads,
Where I'll stop would be the edge, one day I'll have to pay for my deeds,
I've come long from being that kid, who had once left his home to learn,
He had killed a lot of people, and now it was going to be his turn.
And soon it was the beginning of my end; the world started to want me dead,
My demise was the product of the choices I had made.
And I thought about the old days, where has my old world crept?
It had no one but my mother, with the little time she had left.
Now when the fight was over, and I had the longest scar,
Having roamed around the world, I craved return back to her
Even she kept her share of horrors, but she had kept her world pure,
If she sees what I have become, would she still be able to endure?
She is breathing what she can, although her eyes hardly have light,
But I can't return and risk this life, at least this I have to do right.

Pankaj, I had no soul to call my own, no place call home,
And now I began to run,
And ever since I've been running, I was sure I'll never be done!
Pankaj, ten years; ten years it has been,
What I ate I couldn't tell, where I slept I didn't know,
Was running my role in life; was there nowhere I could go?
Now, I often sit back and contemplate, as those days I recall,
What did I want from life? Where was I running to after all?
And then one day it flashed before my eyes, the realization, at last,
I wasn't running to anything, Pankaj, I was running away from my class.
The compliance to the violence, and every struggle I ever bore
Were all because I didn't want to stay a middle class anymore!
You know, we shouldn't even try escaping; rather embrace it with pride,
Because this entire place is the middle, there's nothing on either side.
It's this maze we are in, and we are told to find the doors,
While in real there are none, it's just the walls, ceilings, and floors!
You are walking through this flawed road; it doesn't matter where it bends,
It just takes you to another one, and this cycle never ends.

Pankaj, I've stopped running now, I know I belong to this maze,
And it's here with that spark I'll spend my remaining days.
It can't tell you where I am, the enemies follow my trails,
I am sure before you reach me I will be found by someone else.
But Pankaj, I never thought I'll make a friend, but I did, and I'm glad.
It's just this once I'll write to you, and we'll never meet, and it's sad
Because I will be waiting by this window, and one day the light would grow dull,
Those bullets would start coming back; they would be drilling through my skull.
It's this simple at the end, you live and you die,
And there's just this little story in the middle. My dear friend, goodbye!</p>
