So, let me begin with the stern proclamation that I am not someone who believes that a mirror would better help survival than a water canteen if ever thrown to a lone desert. But, at times, I do care about how I look; especially when I am about to be interviewed!

So, on that fine Saturday morning, as I enter the elevator in the dimly lit basement I am deeply relieved to find a body length mirror occupying the entire inner wall. And knowing the ability of an hour long bus journey in Kolkata to turn your delighted look to a dishevelled one; the glass lined wall surely seems a heavenly gesture to save my day. Yet as I look myself up the mirror I can no longer identify the charming angel who had left her house. All I find is a girl looking back at me with awkwardly inclined spectacles. Her hair is a mess. And a moment later I realize, painfully, that in fact, she is me!

But the respite is short-lived. As the lift ascends to the second floor, and as I am adjusting the sleeves of my churidar, trying to embed a professional tinge to my terribly unprofessional self, the ascent abruptly stops; and enters a man, probably in mid forties. I say man because he really appears one. And as he is standing next to me I feel as if the walls are suddenly closing in; and I also realize how short I am. And I truly hate when I have such realizations.

The elevator opens to a long corridor with a marbled floor; and then I observe that the walls are marbled too. And it's polished to such an extent that you can make out an obscure reflection of yourself as you are walking by them.

The corridor ends into an overwhelmingly decorated and gigantic lobby initiated by a reception cubicle. The furniture appears like priceless antiques rescued from former royal palaces. And I take a moment to process the entire scenery. I am welcomed by an extremely well dressed lady with an extremely beautiful smile, who asks me to wait in the sofas that seat several others like me; candidates. Not often in my life have I come across glamour from such close range. And then, as I do, I feel like it's, umm, awesome!?

The few yards walk from the reception to the waiting area, having every eye in that lobby on me, feels like a ramp in a lingerie ad. And all of a sudden I am horribly nervous and self conscious. I wonder whether my hips are swinging way too much; or whether the dupatta is hiding what it needs to hide, and yet not hiding too much; and blah blah. But, finally, I make it; eureka.

As I sit I eagerly look around to have a quick study of my fellow competitors; most of which, I find to my surprise, are girls. And then when I am convinced that, typically, there are way too many differences between their appearances; and also in the way they bit their nails; I decide to search the similarities.

And indeed that turns out to be an easier feat; as I find that a majority of them, unlike me, were in formal suits. And this sends a chill of inferiority down my spine. And secondly, I find most of the girls appeared only as tall as me, even after having their curvy bodies elevated by three inches by heels or wedges. And for a moment I recollect the painful memory of the previous time I had tried one of them.

And then the third similarity interrupts the chain of my thoughts. And I realize that everyone in that lobby looked shockingly similar in another particular way. And a second later I realize that all of them looked rich; annoyingly rich.

And amongst the randomly ignited conversations which were overlapping from all around, the girl in front catches my attention. And the guy beside me, in black formals, extends his hands and then engages in something which can truly be referred to as a warm handshake. Promit Roy, I hear him say and I feel that I am utterly left out. I feel that I'm repulsive; maybe because my hips really had weird swings as I walked in.

Meanwhile, I notice that my legs are shaking; vigorously. And it feels awkward when I stop doing so, and I carry on with it. And while I am doing so I hear a word originating at my side. And I realize that it's a hi.

I turn towards him and spend a couple of seconds wondering if his eyes are originally orange. And when I am convinced that I was seeing it wrong I try to look for a comeback for my stupidity. And then, obviously, I smile.

Is it your first time? He asks in a deep voice and I wonder if it has something to do with my virginity.

No, I mean yes. I reply pretending like I don't know that I have miserably failed at my try to not appear confused.

Yeah, and he gazes into my legs and adds a smile to his composure, I can see that.

You, I ask, your first time too?

No. He says and stares deep into my eyes; so deep that I can almost feel him touching my retina.

So, where are you from? I ask him in an attempt to avoid awkward silences.

I live nearby.

Meanwhile a man enters the lobby and takes a position which clearly suggests that he is about to make an announcement. And I realize that it is the man from the elevator. And he relays with total eloquence, you must know that this interview is for university scholarship. And you should also know that there is one among you, the best one, who would be entitled to absolutely free education. And to find who it is, we are about to begin the interviews shortly. Hope you are all set to portray your best self!

And his departure is as abrupt as his arrival; and after he leaves I find that the vibration in my legs has taken larger amplitudes. And the gravity of the announcement accelerates my heartbeats; and it seems like the tension piling up inside me could lead to an explosion at any moment.

And at the same time, I become envious of this guy who is sitting just a few inches away. I doubt whether I have seen such calmness, even in any of the parks I have recently visited. And in such a moment of crisis, all I wish is to find is someone like me.

I look around once again; this time with awfully competitive eyes. And I set myself with a mental note that everyone in that lobby has stolen a Cadbury from my school bag; at least once in their lifetime. And now is the time for vengeance.

But, of course, by everyone I mean everyone except him.

But, indeed, it is true that I am the one who needs this scholarship, badly; at least more badly than anyone else; because I am the only one who is not appearing annoyingly rich. And the worst any of the bastards in the lobby can do is deprive someone of the much-needed aid. And I resolute to not make that happen.

Meanwhile, the lady with the amazing smile turns up again; this time to only announce the order in which we would be summoned for the interview. I am comforted knowing that my turn would come in the latter half &mdash; the eighteenth; and Promit, the twenty first.

But, my body doesn't obey my wishes. With every passing individual I become more nervous than what I've previously been. And as my turn approaches I gradually begin to expect some weird breed of animal, may be even a dinosaur, waiting for me behind the doors of my interview room. Yes, this aptly estimates the level of my nervousness. And in the middle of everything I find myself in a miserable mix of feelings. I find that I am dreaded to decide which of the needs is more urgent &mdash; the need to eat, or the need to pee!

And unexpectedly, like always, I end up striking a conversation; in an attempt to calm myself down.

You said you live nearby, right? I mutter without turning towards him. And later I realize that my words were mislaid in the air. It went nowhere.

But then he surprises me with, only if you are asking that to me.

Damn, this guy is treading my esteem. And I look with the corner of my eyes making sure that he is not looking at me. No, actually, I just wanted to know if there is any restaurant nearby.

There sure is. He replies instantly.

I think I'd have to rush once I finish with this. My stomach is pleading. And I wonder if he is silently appreciating the personification.

Absolutely, he smiles, you must. And he continues, you leave the building, take a right and walk straight. In the immediate cutting take a right again and make sure that you are walking towards St. Xavier's. And once you cross the college take the first left; and a few steps would take you to the place I am talking about.

I take a moment and ask again, right, right, Xavier's and left? Is that how it is?

Absolutely.

The food must be delicious there, isn't it? I enquire wondering whether I'd be able to afford it.

He takes a moment and then replies; very slowly and calmly, hey, trust me; it's the best one in Kolkata.

And the price; is it reasonable?

Yes. And I nod trying to guess where his reason range might fall.

I am counting on you.

My pleasure, he smiles and I realize that my bladder is about to blast. I immediately excuse myself and rush to washroom.

While coming back I cross the seventeenth person who was being carried to the interview room. But, I must admit that the recent conversation had actually lightened me up. And this time I walk comfortably; return his smile with a more pleasant one; and take my seat.

If you don't know already, the scholarship is also means based. He informs as I settle down in my seat; and I shoo the words away until I process again what I just heard.

And I abruptly speak out, what?

Means cum merit; you'll get the scholarship only if you need it. What else do you think they asked the income certificates of our parents for?

And I understand how stupid I have been in not deducing the obvious. Are you sure? I ask to confirm.

You should have checked the prospectus, which I obviously didn't. And fortunately, that was the most reassuring information I have received on the day.

So when my name is called out I walk out with longer strides and more confident steps. I remember him gesturing me with his thumb held up and decorating his face with a soothing wide grin. And as I walk out of the lobby, I am convinced that this guy, Promit Roy, was way more relieving than the body length mirror from the elevator.

The worst thing about an interview is the fact that you never know how it went; because you can never predict what the interviewer wants from you; even if you and the interviewer shared an elevator in the morning. So if someone claims that it went good or well, even great; you would have to conclude that either they have just appeared how they wanted to appear; not how the interviewer wanted them to; or simply, they are lying.

Luckily, it is the first one for me.

And sadly, as I return to the lobby I can't spot my reason of being lucky. Promit Roy, the twenty first, is gone.

I reach out to a few people beginning with the girl in front; enquiring about where he is. But no one seems to have a clue. And it appears like he didn't even exist in the lobby. And to my greater regret, the twenty-first candidate is marked absent when the authority is convinced that he had abandoned the interview. And I conclude, Promit is gone!

So, not to my surprise, I have to welcome this ominous thought; that I'll never meet this person again. And my disappointment leads to hunger. And I walk out of the building mumbling right, right, Xavier's, left in my mind.

And as I consciously try to walk across the street, I can't help myself but think about this guy. I think about how screwed up I was and how he had lifted me up. Maybe anybody could have come up with that; since it was a pretty little conversation that lifted me up. And maybe it was my fault that I was not habituated to talking to people. And maybe Promit Roy wasn't that special at all. And maybe all this time I was doing nothing, but just over thinking. And maybe I am a sentimental stupid!

And in the mean time I cross a number of restaurants and food stalls; engaging undeniable smells of the intricate masalas. And my stomach starts to plead; rather literally on this occasion. And I expect the one he mentioned to be really good; or to put it more appropriately, I expect it to justify his words. And then I cross Xavier's.

As I take a left, I can indeed see a food stall; not a one with glass lined walls and extravagant mannequins at the entrance; like the one I had imagined. It was in fact a little potable stall; using the shed of a tree to good advantage; beneath which laid are the benches that sit a number of customers. And almost immediately and involuntarily I relate it to the lobby where I had been sitting all these while. And the contrast in the aura and the mood is far more significant than the contrast in the physical orientation of the place.

And as I take a few more steps forward I find a tall guy in a kurta, sleeves folded, assuming the behind of the stall. And he looks at me, and I almost shout, Promit?

Had any problem finding the best stall in Kolkata? And I believe he has not stopped grinning from the moment I left for the interview; because it is the same identical grin; exactly the same one.

And I try to make sense of whatever is happening. The day already had too much under its hat. And then, I know that even more are on the way.

Why did you leave, is all I manage to say.

Because leaving was necessary, he replies without disturbing his smile, making that even possible. And it still doesn't make sense as I keep standing there, dumbstruck, trying to figure out an explanation.

And suddenly it strikes me; like a huge thunderstruck; right on the top of my head.

Hey?

Yes.

You said that the scholarship was also means based.

Yes.

So, I mean, did you walk out because&hellip;

No wonder my dad doesn't make much with this little stall. And I don't know how he pulls that off! Speaking and smiling at the same time.

But, why? And both of us fall silent.

Hey, you don't have to worry. I came back with what I always want to have.

And that being?

A customer! He paused. You know, I always do get a customer.

You have done this before? I ask him remembering him say that he wasn't appearing for the first time.

Precisely, madam. And he comes up with this more beautiful and gratifying smile. And I wonder who should be one actually grateful.

But before I can say anything, he cuts me off, madam; I think it's time that I take orders from my customer; and you're the best one I ever had.
