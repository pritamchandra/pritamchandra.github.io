To some is given the yoke of loneliness, to some is given fear. Although it may not seem like it, but you will bear it fine. 

The other day in the metro, some folks have thrown themselves before the train. Some say two lovers died, some say they were students, some say it was just one person. Everybody talks about it as if they know something. The rickshaw driver scoots to make space for me in his seat, and he brings it up unprompted. A strange excitement pervades the air.

When I return to the university I am walking my usual walk by the sunken field. The sky is downcast. And so am I. Dense trees line up the other side. I have been told I walk in a hurry, but this time I am stopped at my tracks when those trees catch my eye.
Some green, some dark blue, some gray, pale yellow  . . .  they are lined up perfectly from the dark to the light, or, from the light to the dark, like in a catalogue of a paint brand. The edges are blended to form an unexpected continuum of colors and leaves. The surreal sight has me stopped for good. When I finally make a move, a quiet inward cheer is nudging my steps. I think I should return to take a photo. 

I return the next day. It is sunny. The colors look incoherent, arbitrary.
