The Mall road is where I am headed this evening. The hotel cars are expensive, so I would either take a bus or a cheap cab down the road. At a curvy turn I settle. The climb is steep. A few buses pass by without stopping.

Eventually other passengers join me. Waiting is always easier in company. Then, around the corner, an unlikely sight of a bicycle emerges. The figure is revealed from the top to the bottom with a dramatic slowness, so much so that two wayfarers simply walk past the bike. But wait, the rider  . . .  is a very old man. His exhausted stand-pedalling is barely moving him, for all he can give that machine is the weight of his own body. As he approaches, he is squinting at the floor due to the dripping sweat, but also due to his great resolve. His passing is like a spectacle, like the sight of a pilgrim. 

In another few minutes, bored by the inactivity, I negotiate with a local driver and leave the waiting crowd. The car catches up with the old man in no time who, still at it, is oscillating upright like a sedated metronome.

Later as I walk uphill on the mall road I see another man – poor, gaunt, tattered clothes. His shoulders are so drooped and rounded that he appears to be lacking his arms. But the arms are intact, pushing uphill a baby carriage, again, no quicker than a snail. It is strange that the struggle, be it ever so slightly, has the appearance of an act. When I pass him my surprise is surely more than what I express. Far ahead I keep stopping to take peeks at the bizarre show. The carriage is empty.
