Lent begins today. The heavens are overcast. It rained after quite a while. I lament the passage of the winter. This winter was harsh. Many days I couldn't get out of bed, and when I did, my body was stiff as a stick, on the verge of snapping. Cold currents seeped through the underside of the bathroom windows, and I could barely use the toilet. This caused other physiological problems. Let me not even get started with the psychological ones, the thorns of my flesh, although I doubt the winter had anything to do with it. Nonetheless, I lament the passage of this winter. This day lacks warmth, and yet just a shirt has sustained me. I lament the necessity of warmth. I lament the desire for it.

How different is evil from inconvenience? Can one lament evil? The Israelites did so in the wilderness. But, perhaps, that was a lapse in their judgment –– they twisted their memories, and remembered slavery as peace. But, can one truly recognize evil, and still lament it? Those who weep in hell, what do they desire? Nothing scares me more than hell. I understand it as the “theoretical worst” (theory, in my sense, is not shy of reality. If anything, it is hyper-real.) Then, heaven, the “theoretical best”, is the one that rescues from the “theoretical worst”. 

> He lifted me out of the slimy pit, <br> out of the mud and mire; <br> he set my feet on a rock <br> &emsp; &emsp; &emsp; &emsp; &emsp; and gave me a firm place to stand. <br> &emsp; &emsp; &emsp; &emsp; &emsp; (Psalm 40)

This lent I wish to pray in writing. So that I may not fail, I generously give myself two days for one prayer. May God give me the words. I know my flesh will push for the words to be good. Maybe some lent I will give up that desire. But this lent I desire my words to be true. In its truth may it have no blemish. Some lent, maybe, I will also give up my lust. Some lent, maybe, I will despise the mirror.

Today, I thank God for the winter that passed. The winter was smeared with loss, but why overlook the gain. Why overlook the many joys, moments of purpose and peace, of pleasure. Why overlook the scarce and invaluable moments of love. Why overlook the aesthetic of sweaters and jackets, and the surreal face of fog. The season is changing, and the seasons are unchanging. I am grateful to God for change. For respite from stagnation, for variety and multiplicity. I am grateful to God for permanence, and constancy. Thank you Jesus for the seasons never fail to come.
